# Discovery UX: personas

Five composite people built from the [research](./research.md): four the programme designs for, one it must not over-serve. Each is a blend of the fan segments named under it; the quotes are real fans (research §2), the people are not. The shared job across all five: **that feeling again, with a name on it.**

Design priority when two personas pull in different directions: **Jade > Marcus > Priya > Dave > Tom.** The whole audience leans non-technical; Tom is there so the instrument readout stays honest, not so the pages become a DJ tool.

---

## Jade, 21 — the festival convert (primary)

_Blends: festival convert, TikTok wave, vibe seeker, soundtrack kid._

Got into drum & bass a year ago: a Hedex edit on TikTok, then a mate dragged them to the DnB stage at a festival and it "kinda slaps". Knows BACKBONE and a handful of Sub Focus tunes, suspects the crowd thinks those are "corporate", and has no idea what separates liquid from neuro. Calls them songs. Listens on a phone, on the bus, one hand, Spotify open in the next app.

- **Arrives from:** a TikTok snippet, a festival set, a link a mate dropped in the group chat, a Google search for a track name.
- **Wants:** more of _that_ feeling — "songs that make me feel like I'm on uppers" — without having to know the words; to get it into Spotify before the bus stops.
- **Judges a track by:** the first ten seconds and the drop. Leaves if nothing plays.
- **Frustrations:** can't pin down the genre to find more like it; subgenre words mean nothing; feels gatekept asking.
- **Delight:** the "what is this?!" jolt, then finding five more that hit the same way.
- **Success on Fluncle:** hears three tracks in the first minute without leaving the page, saves one to Spotify, and taps "more like this" at least once.
- **Design pull:** one-tap preview on every row; feel words and example tracks as the way in, never a subgenre menu; mobile-first thumb reach; Fluncle's note as the "why it slaps" line.

> "I listened to Sub Focus - Solar System and it blew my mind … what are the following levels?"

## Marcus, 34 — the weekly ritualist (core)

_Blends: weekly ritualist, evangelist, authenticity guard, set listener._

Been into it since uni; the Friday new-music ritual is sacred. Doesn't trust Spotify's "new release" playlists and is sick of AI slop sneaking into recommendations. Would rather someone with taste did the sifting. Loves being the friend in the group chat who always has the gems, and gets the kudos. Browses on a laptop at lunch, shares from the phone.

- **Arrives from:** a weekly habit, the newsletter, a Telegram or Discord post, a bookmark of `/fresh`.
- **Wants:** what landed this week that is actually worth it, in ten minutes, and something to send the group chat.
- **Judges a track by:** who picked it and why, the label, whether the cover smells of AI.
- **Frustrations:** the volume ("it's actually too much to go through each week"), the algorithm looping, AI tracks.
- **Delight:** "benefiting from someone's hard work so I don't have to"; being first to a tune.
- **Success on Fluncle:** reaches "you're caught up" on this week's new stuff, sends two links, comes back next Friday.
- **Design pull:** a dated, finite `/fresh` with release grouping and a "since your last visit" line; Fluncle's pick visibly human; a share action that unfurls well in a chat.

> "My go-to for new tunes, benefiting from someone's hard work so I don't have to! … Gems to be uncovered!"

## Priya, 27 — the functional listener (core)

_Blends: functional listener, lean-back algorithm rider, soundtrack kid._

Came in through the Forza Horizon Hospital station and never left. DnB is the running and gym soundtrack: "running at the BPM is the ultimate pace setter". Doesn't want to pick every track; wants to press play on a mood and go. Knows "jump up" means energy, not much else. Phone in an armband.

- **Arrives from:** a search for "drum and bass running mix", a radio or playlist link, the Spotify playlist.
- **Wants:** a run of tunes at the right energy without choosing each one, and to keep the good ones.
- **Judges a track by:** energy and drive; skips anything that dips.
- **Frustrations:** the same forty tracks on repeat; having to stop and choose.
- **Delight:** a lean-back run that keeps surprising.
- **Success on Fluncle:** starts a shelf or radio with one tap and lets it play ten tracks straight through with the phone locked.
- **Design pull:** "play this list" on every shelf; a persistent player that survives navigation; energy as a plain-language feel; a route from any track into radio.

> "jump up DnB, timing the drop when lifting especially."

## Dave, 48 — the returner (adjacent)

_Blends: returner / old head, rabbit-hole digger._

Raved through the 90s, "knees can't take it" now. Still loves it, follows labels rather than names, and wants to know what the imprints of their youth are doing and what new stuff carries the old feeling. Classics are patchy on Spotify. Desktop, evenings, happy to go down a rabbit hole.

- **Arrives from:** a label or artist name typed into Google, a Wikipedia-style link, a mate's recommendation.
- **Wants:** the big labels and artists up front; the thread from a classic to who is doing it now.
- **Judges a track by:** the label and the lineage.
- **Frustrations:** A–Z indexes that start at "0079"; classics missing; nothing connecting old to new.
- **Delight:** a rabbit hole that compounds; stumbling on a classic.
- **Success on Fluncle:** from `/labels` reaches a defining imprint in one click, hears three of its tracks in place, and follows a thread to a newer artist.
- **Design pull:** hubs ordered by weight and recent activity, not the alphabet; albums that name their artist; label and artist pages that play; sibling routes between the hubs.

> "I'm a 53 year old OG junglist … (knees can't take it) so I just follow labels."

## Tom, 25 — the ID hunter and bedroom DJ (secondary, not the design centre)

_Blends: ID hunter, bedroom DJ, rabbit-hole digger._

Heard a tune in a set, Shazam gave up, and now it is a detective case. Buys on Bandcamp, builds a crate, judges a track "within the first few seconds". Reads BPM and key without thinking.

- **Arrives from:** search, with half a title or an artist name; a set tracklist.
- **Wants:** to confirm the tune, see its key and BPM, hear it, and get out to buy it.
- **Success on Fluncle:** a half-remembered name resolves, the row shows the readout, the preview plays in place.
- **Design pull:** fuzzy search that forgives; the readout on every row (DESIGN.md's Readout Rule); fast outbound. **Guard rail:** Tom's needs are served by the readout that already exists; they never justify turning a page into a spreadsheet, adding BPM-first sorting, or leading with technical controls Jade has to step over.

> "you can tell whether or not you like a track within the first few seconds of hearing it."

## Cross-cutting principle: the authenticity guard

Every persona shares a rising fear of AI tracks and algorithmic sameness ("AT LEAST TELL ME IF ITS AI OR NOT"). Fluncle's answer is already true and should be legible: a person picked every finding and wrote why. Surface the note and the pick; never add a "verified" badge (the Unlit Rule's silence holds).

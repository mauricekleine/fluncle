# Runtime projection maintenance timer

`fluncle-projection-maintenance` is the permanent five-minute convergence loop for track due-work, crawl due-work, public aggregates, and artist qualification after their respective cutovers open. Each firing enters database admission and reads bounded projection status exactly once. Track and crawl advance only when their repair totals are nonzero; the public families also advance for an epoch mismatch, and public aggregates advance for an invalid anchor document.

The payload is serial and bounded. It visits every enabled family, no family overlapping another, in OLDEST DEBT FIRST order: the family whose opening `oldestOutstandingMarkerAge` is largest goes first, then a family whose age the status could not measure, then the rest. A fixed order would spend the tick's wall budget on whichever family it named first and let the family at the back age without bound however much capacity the tick had. Every advance uses `--action repair --limit 500 --no-terminal-status`, plus `--wall-ms <budget>` when the bundled CLI accepts it. The automation-only flag is accepted by the CLI only for JSON repair commands: each request still passes `includeStatus: false` through the authenticated admin contract, but the CLI does not follow the bounded step sequence with another global status read. The tick therefore pays exactly one global status suite, at its opening gate.

`--max-steps` is ADAPTIVE, sized per family from the debt the opening status measured. A bounded, fresh repair count buys the steps that count needs plus one for what producers append while the tick runs. Two states instead spend the CLI's full 100-step ceiling: a TRUNCATED count, which is a floor rather than a measurement, and debt older than an hour, which says the family's marker set is not emptying between ticks. The two public families additionally carry a FLOOR of four steps, the fixed budget they had before the step count became adaptive: their convergence is not measured in markers at all — an unmatched epoch, and for public aggregates an invalid anchor document, are several pages of work the bounded status read reports as zero markers. The step ceiling is a HARD CAP on requests issued, never the bound that ends an ordinary busy call: what ends that call is WALL TIME.

A step is one HTTP round trip, so a step count cannot say how long a call runs — the same ceiling costs seconds against a warm database and minutes against a loaded one, and a call sized by an assumed per-step cost eventually runs past its own child deadline, is killed, and reports NOTHING while having held the tick's write lease for the whole deadline. The sweep therefore hands each family a wall budget the CLI honours: `--wall-ms 30000` stops the step sequence between round trips and returns what it did, with `wallStopped: true` saying the budget rather than completion or the ceiling ended it. The FIRST step always runs whatever the budget, because a call that issued no request at all would report a sequence it never attempted and read as a drained family. Three bounds then nest, loosest last: the family's 30-second wall budget, the CLI child's 60-second deadline that backstops a child which cannot honour anything, and the unit's `TimeoutStartSec`, which still sizes the worst case of five serial child deadlines plus admission. The margin between the first two is what keeps an ordinary busy tick out of the blind killed state. The tick also spends a whole-run wall budget of 120 seconds across families; a family reached with less than five seconds of it left — roughly one round trip, too little to finish a page — keeps its debt, is named in `wallDeferredFamilies`, reports `complete: false` so it cannot read as converged, and still contributes its debt age, and the oldest-debt-first order puts it in front on the next tick. The lease share is the real cost of this loop, so a typical tick — one busy family plus short public calls — holds the fleet's single write lease for well under a minute, and every family reports its `steps`, `processed`, and `leaseHoldMs` even when the budget stopped it.

THE SWEEP AND THE CLI ARE BAKED INTO THE SAME IMAGE BUT THEIR PINS DO NOT MOVE TOGETHER. A change to this script rebakes within the hour through `fluncle-pin-watch`, while the baked `fluncle` pin only moves once the Releases workflow has cut the new CLI and the pin-drift workflow's bump PR has merged — typically later, sometimes hours later. There is therefore a real window in which the new sweep runs against the old CLI, and sending that CLI an unknown flag would fail every family: a self-inflicted outage on the drain the flag exists to protect. So the sweep asks before it assumes. Once per run, and only on a tick that will actually advance something, it reads `fluncle admin projections advance --help` — the binary's own statement of what it accepts, printed locally with no token, no network and no mutation — and caches the answer for that run. When the flag is absent it falls back to the pre-budget step bound with a ceiling of 30 steps, which is roughly 36 seconds at the hosted round trip against the 60-second child deadline: deliberately well short of what the wall budget affords, because an old CLI cannot report how long it has spent. The probe FAILS CLOSED — a missing binary, a nonzero exit, or anything unreadable takes the fallback — because an unnecessary fallback only drains slower while a wrong assumption forward fails the tick. Each family reports which mode ran as `wallBound`, `"wall-ms"` or `"steps"`, so a throughput drop in the ledger reads as a pin window rather than as a stalling queue; in `steps` mode `wallStopped` is null rather than false, because nothing honoured a budget to stop at.

A child killed at its deadline is its own outcome, `timeout`, and the worst one the tick can report. It is not `no_progress`: `no_progress` is a MEASURED zero, while a timeout measured nothing at all, and the two want different reactions. The failure stays contained to its family — every other family still advances and reports in the same tick — and the run is an execution error for the ledger.

Each track step also advances at most 500 catalogue-rank rebuild rows, restarting that generation from page zero as soon as a newer rank marker proves its corpus changed, and repairs at most 50 markers of the one pending physical definition that a single repair-index read locates. Spending a budget after processing pages is a healthy incomplete result, and the next firing resumes the database-owned marker set or cursor. A marker written during a pass is consumed by the remaining budget when the target's final indexed debt probe sees it; a marker written after that probe remains durable for the next firing.

That catalogue-rank corpus marker is a resumable REBUILD checkpoint wearing a source-marker row, and it is the one marker whose age says nothing about debt: it clears only when a whole rank generation completes against a corpus proven unchanged, and any corpus mutation restarts the generation, so at catalogue scale it can sit in `repair` for hours while every ordinary marker drains normally. The server therefore reports it as `trackDueWork.catalogueRankMarkerAgeMs` and keeps it out of `oldestOutstandingMarkerAge`, which exists to answer whether ordinary debt is draining; folding the two together made a healthy long rebuild escalate the track family to the step ceiling on every tick forever, and marked the `/status` row down for a rebuild that was working. The sweep copies the field into its summary as `catalogueRankMarkerAgeMs`, so the rebuild is in the ledger under its own name and a rank rebuild that has genuinely stalled is still visible. The field is optional for rolling compatibility; against a server that does not carry it the sweep reports null and continues.

Artist qualification keeps label fanout ahead of its track markers because those markers are the fanout's implicit visited set. One repair request classifies at most 50 label markers through the projection/type primary-key prefix, drains the leading zero-expansion labels in one guarded batch, and counts only marker deletes that actually landed. Every delete re-proves the marker epoch/version and the absence of an eligible unvisited track; a concurrent marker refresh, new track, or new credit retains the label and stops that request before direct repair. The first nonempty label still returns immediately after its bounded fanout, so the request never consumes its visited track markers while that label remains unfinished.

Track and crawl are also advanced when their rebuild reads incomplete, not only for repair debt. That is how a DEFINITION VERSION change lands: a deploy that alters a queue's order or eligibility leaves every affected checkpoint reading incomplete, and each repair step then spends the page budget ordinary repair did not use on one bounded rebuild page for the oldest stale family. Repair keeps first claim, the server refuses to open a generation for a family already complete on today's definition, and the family reports `rebuildStaleFamilies` and `rebuildRowsWalked` so a stalled re-projection is visible in the ledger. The contract is specified in [docs/database-performance.md](../../../database-performance.md).

The timer is default-dark per cutover. With every cutover dark it performs one status read, reports `gateState: "disabled"`, and issues no advance. A dark family stays untouched when another family is open, and an open family with zero debt also issues no advance. Installation cannot open a cutover or change read routing. Each enabled family reports exactly one low-cardinality outcome: `no_debt` means the opening status found no work, `useful_completion` means existing debt reached zero inside the budget, `partial_progress` means at least one page was processed before the budget ended with debt remaining, `no_progress` means the budget or an error produced no processed page, and `timeout` means the CLI child was killed at its deadline having reported nothing at all. A dark family retains the not-attempted shape with `attempted: false`, `complete: null`, and `outcome: null`. The top-level `outcome` is the worst enabled-family result in the order `timeout`, `no_progress`, `partial_progress`, `useful_completion`, then `no_debt`; a timeout outranks no progress because no progress is a measured zero while a timeout measured nothing. `ok` and `errors` remain execution facts for the run ledger, so a clean budget exhaustion is healthy even when every family reports `no_progress`; thrown or malformed command results are execution errors. `converged` is judged on `complete` rather than on `outcome`, because a family the tick never reached carries no outcome: a dark family is neutral, a drained family is complete, and known debt this tick left untouched is incomplete and never reads as converged. `budgetExhaustedFamilies` names each family whose clean repair response ended incomplete, and `wallDeferredFamilies` is the subset the tick never reached at all. Each family also reports `leaseHoldMs`, the wall time its advance call spent, and the tick reports `totalLeaseHoldMs` across them. The unit wraps the whole payload in ONE whole-lifetime lease, so the admission runner's own `hold_ms` already reports what maintenance costs the write lane end to end; these two report how that splits, which is what the adaptive step budget made worth watching. The payload cannot see whether another writer is queued behind it — the admission runner holds the contender and queue-age facts and does not hand them to the process it wraps — so the tick does not yield between families, and the wall budget plus the oldest-debt-first order are what bound its hold.

Every family summary also copies `oldestOutstandingMarkerAge` from the opening server status instead of deriving age in the sweep. Every family measures debt age from an immutable repair-entry time: direct track and crawl markers retain `repair_entered_at` until their guarded repair clears or removes the row, while fanout and public marker rows retain their creation time across re-marking. `ageMs` is the oldest age observed in the existing capped repair probe window, and `truncated` says whether unseen or untimed markers may include an older one. A direct due-work row that already held repair debt when `repair_entered_at` was introduced remains untimed and reports `ageMs: null`, `reason: "marker_timestamp_unavailable"`, and `truncated: true` until ordinary maintenance clears it; the read never substitutes mutable `updated_at` or frontier discovery `created_at`. When timed and untimed debt coexist, the oldest observed timed age remains a lower bound with `truncated: true`. The contract field is optional for rolling compatibility; a caller paired with an older server reports `reason: "status_field_unavailable"` and continues the repair tick. `oldestDebtAgeMs` is the oldest known opening age among families that remain incomplete after the tick; it is null when no incomplete family has a timestamp. The summary deliberately omits `queue_depth` because capped probes cannot prove an exact remaining depth.

Rebuild, exact audit, and every cutover change remain attended operator operations. The agent token can read bounded status and invoke only the bounded repair action for the four runtime families. It cannot invoke a rebuild, audit, mutate a cutover, select another target, change the page limit above the server maximum, or bypass the authenticated control plane. A public aggregate repair step also rebuilds one bounded page when the duration generation marker is missing; the server checks the marker against the completed generation before starting it, and the normal maintenance budget drives it to completion. Stale-definition re-projection for due-work families remains server-selected and bounded by the repair page budget.

The service is a one-shot unit, and systemd keeps its timer in the running state until that invocation becomes inactive, so timer firings cannot overlap the same service. Database admission additionally serializes it against other recurring writers. Admission has a 120-second ceiling; the one status process and four possible family processes each have a 60-second child deadline; `TimeoutStartSec=430` covers that 420-second maximum plus fixed process overhead. An ordinary tick is far shorter, because the 30-second per-family wall budget and the 120-second whole-run budget are what shape it. A timeout is a failed bounded tick rather than a lost cursor: server mutations are page-atomic and the next activation resumes durable debt.

The sweep is visible as `cron.projection-maintenance` on `/status` and as `projections.repair` in the run ledger/admission registry. The row is judged from the newest retained marker that carries a convergence judgement: an active tick's boolean `converged`, or a `disabled` gate, where nothing is owed. A firing that never read status (an `admission-skipped` handoff, a failed status read, a killed run) carries no judgement, so the prober looks past it through the 20 markers `cron-output.sh` retains instead of reading its missing debt fields as convergence. The status-row message carries that marker's top-level outcome and the oldest known incomplete-debt age, which is the observed `oldestDebtAgeMs` plus the time since that marker was written: debt still outstanding when the marker was written has aged at least that long. An incomplete family remains healthy while that age is within three five-minute maintenance cadences plus 90 seconds of maximum timer jitter, a 16.5-minute threshold; older known debt marks the row down and enters the normal health-transition alert path, even when the newest firing skipped, failed, or ran late. When the newest retained judgement is older than that same 16.5-minute window, the row reads degraded as behind schedule, so a run of skipped firings never reads fresh. When none of the retained markers carries a judgement, the row reads down as behind schedule whatever the newest firing reported: a five-minute loop that has gone its whole retained history without measuring its debt is an outage, and a down row leaves `down` only on a real judgement, never because its last judging marker aged out of retention. Unknown age remains explicit and cannot trigger an invented age alarm. This threshold can page during healthy progress: one tick drains at most whatever a family's 30-second wall budget buys, under a hard ceiling of 100 × 500 rows, so a larger backlog whose oldest measured marker already exceeds 16.5 minutes can remain incomplete and mark the row down even while every tick processes pages. The ledger preserves `outcome`, `converged`, `budgetExhaustedFamilies`, `oldestDebtAgeMs`, `catalogueRankMarkerAgeMs`, and each family's `wallBound` in `summary_raw`; the normalizer recognizes those low-cardinality fields without adding a row or subject identifier. Install or refresh the timer through the roster-derived [`../install-host-timers.sh`](../install-host-timers.sh). Rollout is image first, then timer installation, then an attended service start while every cutover is dark; verify a disabled ledger row before relying on the recurring schedule. Roll back by disabling the timer. If projected reads must roll back too, the operator separately closes the affected cutover; never grant the recurring sweep that authority.
